title: Lyrics
description: The words and syllables of the soprano, as word, syl, sylpos and melisma.
---

The chorales carry their lyrics as [`**text`](https://www.humdrum.org/rep/text/)
spines under the soprano, one per verse; 353 of the 370 chorales have them. Three
pattern keys read them, and two of them, `word` and `syl`, can drive a search.

| Key       | Value                                                  | Driving feature |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| `word`    | the word at the note, e.g. `"jesu"`                    | yes             |
| `syl`     | the syllable at the note, e.g. `"her"`, the first of `Her-zen` | yes             |
| `sylpos`  | `single`, `initial`, `medial` or `terminal`: where the syllable stands in its word, or MEI's `@wordpos` letter `s`, `i`, `m`, `t` | no |

The lyrics belong to the soprano, like `fb` belongs to the whole chorale:
whichever voice is searched, they are read at the soprano note at the same time.



## Words and syllables

A `**text` token is a syllable, with a hyphen where it continues into the next
syllable or out of the one before: `E-`, `-wig-`, `-keit`. A syllable continues
the word before it when that one ends with a hyphen.

Words and syllables are compared normalized: in small letters, `ä`, `ö`, `ü`
for `Ä`, `Ö`, `Ü`, `ss` for `ß`, and without the punctuation at either end.
`Gott,`, `Gott!` and `Gott` are all `gott`, `Her-` is the syllable `her`. A
pattern value is normalized the same way: `"Jesu"` finds `jesu`.



## Wildcards

A `*` in a value stands for any characters: `"Her*"` finds `Herr`, `Herz` and
`Herzen`, `"*zen"` finds `Herzen` and `Schmerzen`, `"*erz*"` every word with
`erz` in it. Without a `*` the whole word (or syllable) has to be the value:
`"Herz"` does not find `Herzen`. `"*"` alone is any word (or syllable) at all.



## Searching words and syllables

```json
{
    "feature": "word",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "pattern": [{ "word": "Herr" }, { "word": "Jesu" }, { "word": "Christ" }]
}
```

finds every "Herr Jesu Christ" in the corpus. Driven by `word`, the search walks
the words of the soprano one after the other; driven by `syl`, it walks the
syllables. A match starts on the first note of the first word and ends on the
last note of the last word, and a position's `duration` is that of the whole
word (or syllable), all its notes added up. `fermata` and `phrase: "end"` are
judged on the last note of the word, `phrase: "start"` on its first; everything
else at its first note, so `syl` at a `word` position is the first syllable of
the word.

```json
{
    "feature": "syl",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "pattern": [
        { "sylpos": "initial", "melisma": "initial", "duration": { "operator": "greaterThan", "value": "1" } },
        { "sylpos": "terminal", "melisma": "none" }
    ]
}
```

finds the words of two syllables with a melisma of more than a whole note on
the first and a single note on the last: `duration` is that of the whole
syllable, all its notes added up. Driven by `kern`, the two positions would have
to be neighbouring notes; driven by `syl`, the second position is the next
syllable, however many notes the first one takes.



## Melismas

A syllable lasts from its own note over the following notes without a syllable,
until the next syllable, a rest or the end of the phrase: a note with a fermata
ends the melisma, and the notes without a syllable after it have no text. That
is how a verse can be shorter than the melody: the second verse of a bar form
often has text under the repeat only.

`{ "word": "*" }` asks for a syllable at the note: there is none at a rest, or
at the notes after a phrase end the verse has no text for; `{ "!word": "*" }`
finds those. `{ "!word": "gott" }` holds where another syllable is and where
none is.



## Verses

All verses are read at once. A match needs one verse in which every lyrics
value of every position holds: `[{ "word": "Herr" }, { "word": "Jesu" }]`
cannot take `Herr` from verse 1 and `Jesu` from verse 2. Driven by `word` or
`syl`, the search walks the words of each verse; a word on the same notes in
several verses is found once. In the score, the syllables of the verses a match
holds in are marked.

A verse is one `**text` spine: a stanza, or one line of text where the music
repeats, as the two lines of a bar form stand under the same notes.
[chor005](/chorale/chor005) has four: two stanzas of two lines each. A chorale without lyrics has no verse, so nothing
is found in it, not even with a negated value.
