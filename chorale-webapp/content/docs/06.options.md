Query-level fields that don't belong to `pattern` itself: toggles that change
how a match's boundaries or comparisons are computed. They never change *which*
features or values a pattern asks for, only how those asks are judged.

| Option                                                                  | Type                              | Default   | What it does                                                                                              |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [`mintStartAtPreviousToken`](#mintstartatprevioustoken)                 | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Reports a `mint` match as starting on the note the first interval was measured *from*, one onset earlier. |
| [`mintAllowIntervalComplementation`](#mintallowintervalcomplementation) | `string`/`number` or `array`      | —         | Lets the listed interval numbers also match their complement, so `"-5"` can also match `+4`.              |
| [`fbCompareExactChord`](#fbcompareexactchord)                           | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Requires an `fb` chord to have exactly the figures of the pattern value, with no extras.                  |
| [`kernIgnoreOctave`](#kernignoreoctave)                                 | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Matches a `kern` pitch in every register instead of the one written.                                      |
| [`hintReduceCompound`](#hintreducecompound)                             | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Folds `hint` intervals into one octave before comparing, so `"3"` also matches a 10th.                    |
| [`durationAllowSplitNotes`](#durationallowsplitnotes)                   | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Lets a `duration` also be added up from consecutive repetitions of the same note.                         |
| [`durationAllowMergedNotes`](#durationallowmergednotes)                 | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Lets consecutive `duration`s also be divided out of one single longer note.                               |
| [`metweightSkipUnclassified`](#metweightskipunclassified)               | `boolean`                         | `false`   | Treats an onset on an unclassified metric position as an ornament and leaves it out of the search.        |
| [`simultaneousAlignment`](#simultaneousalignment)                       | `"start"`, `"end"`, `"start-end"` | `"start"` | Picks which positions of a `simultaneousWith` match must line up with the primary match.                  |

Every one of them can also be set inside a single entry of
[`simultaneousWith`](/docs/simultaneous), overriding the query's own value just
for that group's matching. Leaving one out of a group inherits the query's
top-level value instead.



## `mintStartAtPreviousToken`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

Only affects searches driven by [`mint`](/docs/features/mint). A `**mint` token
describes the interval *into* a note, so the first onset your pattern checks is
already the second note of the phrase you're matching. The note the interval
starts *from* isn't itself checked by position 0, and by default it's left out
of the reported `startPosition`/`startLine`.

Setting `mintStartAtPreviousToken: true` shifts the reported start back by one
onset, so the match begins on the note the first interval was measured from, not
the note it lands on.

```json
{
    "feature": "mint",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "mintStartAtPreviousToken": true,
    "pattern": [
        { "mint": "-2" },
        { "mint": "-2" }
    ]
}
```

Without the option, a match's `startLine` points at the *second* note of the
descending-2nds run; with it, `startLine` points at the *first* note, one note
earlier, so the highlighted range covers all three notes of the run instead of
just the last two:

::code-group

```vue [without the option]
chorale  feature  voice    start_line  end_line  start_position  end_position
chor029  mint     Soprano  26          30        3               4
chor029  mint     Soprano  30          32        4               5
chor029  mint     Soprano  32          34        5               6
chor029  mint     Soprano  34          36        6               7
chor029  mint     Soprano  46          48        12              13
chor029  mint     Soprano  48          50        13              14
chor029  mint     Soprano  57          60        19              20
chor029  mint     Soprano  60          61        20              21
chor029  mint     Soprano  73          76        27              28
chor029  mint     Soprano  76          78        28              29
chor029  mint     Soprano  78          80        29              30
chor029  mint     Soprano  87          89        34              35
chor029  mint     Soprano  89          90        35              35+1/2
chor029  mint     Soprano  90          92        35+1/2          36
chor029  mint     Soprano  105         107       44              45
chor029  mint     Soprano  107         109       45              46
chor029  mint     Soprano  113         116       48              50
```

```vue [with mintStartAtPreviousToken]
chorale  feature  voice    start_line  end_line  start_position  end_position
chor029  mint     Soprano  25          30        2               4
chor029  mint     Soprano  26          32        3               5
chor029  mint     Soprano  30          34        4               6
chor029  mint     Soprano  32          36        5               7
chor029  mint     Soprano  43          48        11              13
chor029  mint     Soprano  46          50        12              14
chor029  mint     Soprano  56          60        18              20
chor029  mint     Soprano  57          61        19              21
chor029  mint     Soprano  70          76        26              28
chor029  mint     Soprano  73          78        27              29
chor029  mint     Soprano  76          80        28              30
chor029  mint     Soprano  85          89        33              35
chor029  mint     Soprano  87          90        34              35+1/2
chor029  mint     Soprano  89          92        35              36
```

**The escape hatch**: the shift is skipped when position `0` of your pattern
explicitly sets `mint` to a wildcard, `{ "mint": "*" }`, so that it doesn't add
one extra step on top of a position you've already accounted for yourself. Any
other position `0` (a specific `mint` value, or no `mint` key at all, e.g. only
checking `duration` or another feature) still gets the shift.

**Rests**: `**mint` measures across rests, from the last note that actually
sounded, and the shifted start follows that same rule. If one or more rests sit
between the first matched interval and the note it was measured from, the start
skips over them to that sounding note rather than landing on a rest. When
nothing sounds earlier in the voice at all, there is nothing to shift back to
and the match keeps its own first onset.



## `mintAllowIntervalComplementation`

**Type:** `string`/`number` or `array` of `strings`/`numbers` · **Default:** *none* (off)

Affects any [`mint`](/docs/features/mint) pattern key, whether `mint` is the
driving feature or a cross-referenced check alongside another one. It lets a
pattern value *also* be satisfied by its **complementary interval**: the
interval that fills the rest of the octave in the opposite direction. A 5th down
and a 4th up land on the same pitch class from the same starting note, an octave
apart, so for many melodic questions they're the same move:

```json
{
    "feature": "mint",
    "voices": "bass",
    "pattern": [
        { "mint": "-5" }
    ],
    "mintAllowIntervalComplementation": ["5"]
}
```

Without the option, this finds only descending 5ths. With it, it also finds
ascending 4ths.

The value is the set of **diatonic numbers** you're opting in, either a single
value or an array. Entries may be written as strings or as plain numbers (`["4",
"5"]` and `[4, 5]` are the same thing), and `"*"` opts in every number at once.
Only simple intervals have a complement within the octave, so entries must be
`1`–`8` (or `"*"`).

### How a value is complemented

| Component | Complement                                                               |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| number    | `9 −` the number: `1`&harr;`8`, `2`&harr;`7`, `3`&harr;`6`, `4`&harr;`5` |
| direction | flipped: `+`&harr;`−` (a value with no sign stays direction-agnostic)    |
| quality   | inverted: `M`&harr;`m`, `A`&harr;`d`, `P` stays `P` (so `AA`&harr;`dd`)  |

So `"-P5"` also matches `+P4`, `"+M2"` also matches `-m7`, and the sign-less
`"5"` (a 5th in either direction) also matches any 4th in either direction.
Quality is inverted properly, which means the option never widens *quality*: a
pattern of `"+M2"` picks up `-m7`, never `-M7`.

### It's the number in your pattern that opts in

An entry opts in every pattern value **written** with that number, not every
value that could complement *to* it. Listing `"5"` makes `{ "mint": "-5" }` also
match a `+4`; it does **not** make `{ "mint": "+4" }` also match a `-5`. To have
it both ways, list both numbers:

```json
{
    "feature": "mint",
    "pattern": [
        { "mint": "+4" }
    ],
    "mintAllowIntervalComplementation": [4, 5]
}
```

This keeps each direction of the shorthand something the query asks for
explicitly, so a pattern position never quietly matches more than the numbers
you listed.

Values with nothing to complement are left alone even under `"*"`: a value
without a diatonic number (`"+"`, `"-"`, a bare quality), a compound interval (a
9th and up), and `mint`'s bracketed first-note marker (e.g. `"[gg]"`) all keep
matching exactly what they matched before. Negation composes as usual: with the
option on, `{ "!mint": "-5" }` excludes both descending 5ths **and** ascending
4ths (see [Negating a feature](/docs/patterns#negating-a-feature)).

Listing `1`, `8` or `"*"` has one effect beyond comparing pattern values: for a
`mint`-driven search it also lets an octave leap hold a
[`durationAllowSplitNotes`](#durationallowsplitnotes) run together, since the
octave is the unison's complement.



## `fbCompareExactChord`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

Only affects comparisons against [`fb`](/docs/features/fb). By default, a
multi-figure pattern value like `"2 4"` is a **minimum** requirement. It matches
any chord that contains *at least* those figures, so it also matches an actual
chord of `"2 4 6"`. Setting `fbCompareExactChord: true` requires the actual
chord to have **exactly** as many figures as the pattern value, no more:

```json
{
    "feature": "fb",
    "fbCompareExactChord": true,
    "pattern": [
        { "fb": "m6 m3" }
    ]
}
```

With the default (`false`), this also matches a four-note chord that happens to
contain both a minor 6th and a minor 3rd above the bass, alongside other
figures. With `fbCompareExactChord: true`, it only matches chords that are
*exactly* a minor 6th and minor 3rd, nothing else sounding above the bass.



## `kernIgnoreOctave`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

Affects any `kern` pattern key, whether `kern` is the driving feature or a
cross-referenced check alongside another one. By default, a pitch pattern
value's register must match exactly, so `"G"` only matches a `G` in that exact
octave, not `g`, `GG`, `gg`, or any other octave of the same pitch class.
Setting `kernIgnoreOctave: true` matches that pitch class in **every** register:

```json
{
    "feature": "kern",
    "kernIgnoreOctave": true,
    "pattern": [
        { "kern": "G" }
    ]
}
```

Accidentals always count as part of the pitch regardless of the option: `"G#"`
with `kernIgnoreOctave: true` matches a G♯ in any octave, never a plain G.
Rhythm and fermata components in the same pattern value (e.g. `"4G;"`) are
unaffected and still have to match exactly.



## `hintReduceCompound`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

Affects any [`hint-<pair>`/`hint-<voice>`](/docs/features/hint) pattern key. By
default, a pattern value's interval size must match exactly, so `"3"` only
matches a genuine 3rd, not a compound 3rd (a 10th, a 17th, …). Setting
`hintReduceCompound: true` folds both the pattern value and the actual interval
down to within an octave before comparing. A unison and an octave stay distinct
from each other. So `"3"` also matches a 10th:

```json
{
    "feature": "hint-14",
    "hintReduceCompound": true,
    "pattern": [
        { "hint-14": "M3" }
    ]
}
```

Without the option, this only matches an actual major 3rd between bass and
soprano. With it, it also matches a major 10th or major 17th between them, since
bass/soprano is wide enough that the interval is routinely compound in the raw
data (see [hint](/docs/features/hint#token-format)).



## `durationAllowSplitNotes`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

Affects any `duration` pattern key. By default a position's `duration` is the
sounding duration of the **one** onset that position is checked against, so a
pattern asking for a half note only finds notes actually written as half notes.
A chorale often spells the same musical note out as repeated shorter notes
instead and those repetitions are missed.

Setting `durationAllowSplitNotes: true` lets such a position instead be
satisfied by a **run of consecutive onsets that re-articulate one and the same
note**, whose durations add up to one of the position's allowed values:

```json
{
    "feature": "kern",
    "voices": "alto",
    "durationAllowSplitNotes": true,
    "pattern": [
        { "kern": "d", "duration": "2" }
    ]
}
```

In chor001 the alto's opening `d` is a quarter-note upbeat re-attacked as
another quarter in bar 1; A half note's worth of `d`, never written as one.
Without the option only the genuine half notes are found; with it, the split one
joins them.


### What counts as the same note

A run may only grow across onsets that re-attack the note it started on, which
depends on the driving feature:

| Driving feature | A run continues while                                                                     |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `kern`          | the pitch stays the same (rhythm, beams, slurs and ties are ignored)                      |
| `mint`          | the interval into the onset is a perfect unison (`P1`) — how `**mint` writes a repetition |
| anything else   | the spine repeats its own token (`deg`, `fb`, `metweight`, `hint-<pair>`)                 |

A run also stops as soon as the sum **overshoots** every allowed value: with
`"duration": "2"`, three repeated quarters are a dotted half and match nothing,
while the first two of them are a match.

### Octave leaps under `mintAllowIntervalComplementation`

For a `mint`-driven search, a perfect octave holds a run together just as a
unison does, provided the query listed the unison-octave pair itself (`1`, `8`
or `"*"`) in
[`mintAllowIntervalComplementation`](#mintallowintervalcomplementation), since
`P8` is `P1`'s complement. chor009's bass cadences that way four times: the
dominant is a half note's worth written as two quarters an octave apart
(quarters 13–15: `4D`, `4DD`), stepping to the tonic under the fermata (`4GG;`,
written `+P4`). Here `"*"` does two jobs, `8` for the octave re-attack and `5`
so that `"-P5"` reaches that `+P4`; `[5, 8]` would do as well:

```json
{
    "feature": "mint",
    "voices": "bass",
    "durationAllowSplitNotes": true,
    "mintAllowIntervalComplementation": "*",
    "pattern": [
        { "duration": "2" },
        { "mint": "-P5", "fermata": true }
    ]
}
```

This is a `mint` rule only. A `kern`-driven run always needs the identical
pitch, `kernIgnoreOctave` included; that option decides how a pattern *value* is
compared, not what holds a run together.


### The rest of the position

Only `duration` is summed. Every **other** key of the same position is checked
on **every** onset of the run, so a `deg`, `fb` or `hint-<pair>` constraint has
to hold for the whole logical note, not just its first onset; a repetition under
changing harmony fails a harmonic constraint that the change breaks. Two keys
are treated specially:

- The **driving feature's own key** is checked on the run's first onset only.
  What follows is a re-attack of that same note, which is what the table above
  already vouches for.
- **`fermata`** is checked on the run's **last** onset, the one that closes the
  note off, since that is where a chorale writes the fermata.

A run consumes as many onsets as it needs, and the pattern simply carries on
from the one after it. So positions after a split position line up with the
notes that actually follow the run, and a match's `endLine`/`endPosition` report
the last onset it consumed.



## `durationAllowMergedNotes`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

The converse of [`durationAllowSplitNotes`](#durationallowsplitnotes), and
independent of it. Affects any `duration` pattern key. By default every position
of a pattern needs an onset of its own, so a pattern spelling a cadence out as
three quarters only finds voices that actually write three quarters. a voice
that ties the first two together into a half note is missed, even though it is
singing the same thing.

Setting `durationAllowMergedNotes: true` lets a **single onset satisfy several
consecutive positions at once**, as long as their durations add up to exactly
that onset's sounding duration:

```json
{
    "feature": "kern",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "pattern": [
        { "kern": "a", "duration": "4" },
        { "kern": "a", "duration": "4" },
        { "kern": "g", "duration": "4", "fermata": "true" }
    ],
    "durationAllowMergedNotes": true
}
```

In chor009 the soprano's bar 4 is a half note `a` and then the fermata `g`,
where alto, tenor and bass move in quarters. Without the option the query finds
nothing there; with it, the half note answers the pattern's first two positions
and the fermata `g` the third.


### How the onset is divided up

The durations of the run's positions must sum to the onset's sounding duration
**exactly**. A quarter plus a quarter is a half note, a quarter plus a half is
not. When a position offers several values (`"duration": ["4", "8"]`), the
shortest run of positions that adds up exactly is the one taken. A run stops as
soon as it would overshoot, and it can never reach across a position without a
concrete `duration` (a missing one, or `"*"`), since such a position has no
share of the onset to claim.

A run of a single position is just the ordinary case: that position's `duration`
has to be the onset's own.


### The rest of the positions

Every position of the run describes the same one onset, so all of their other
keys are checked against it, including `fermata`, which that onset either
carries or doesn't. Two kinds of key are treated specially, both for the same
reason: a position after the run's first one describes a **re-attack that the
merged note doesn't have**, so the onset's own token is the wrong thing to judge
it against.

- A [`mint`](/docs/features/mint) key on such a position is judged against what
  that re-attack *would* have been: a unison, since it re-articulates the note
  the run started on. Nothing in the score is consulted for it. So `{ "mint":
  "P1" }` is the way to spell the repetition out, and something like `{ "mint":
  "-P5" }` can never hold: there is no second attack inside a merged note for a
  fifth to be measured into. An octave (`"P8"`) passes for a re-attack too, on
  the same opt-in the [split option asks
  for](#octave-leaps-under-mintallowintervalcomplementation): `"1"`, `"8"` or
  `"*"` in `mintAllowIntervalComplementation`.

- A [`kern`](/docs/features/kern) key on such a position keeps its **pitch**
  judged exactly as anywhere else: a re-attack re-articulates the note the run
  started on, so its pitch *is* the merged onset's. Only the **rhythm** written
  into the value is dropped, since the onset's duration is the whole merged
  note's rather than this position's share of it. Constrain the share with
  `duration`, which is what the run divides the onset up by. So in `{ "kern":
  "4a" }` the `a` still has to hold and the `4` is ignored.

Both rules are deliberately **independent of the driving feature**. Asking for
the same repetition means the same thing whether the search runs on `kern`,
`mint`, `deg` or anything else.

Every other key is checked against the onset like any of the run's first
position's: a re-attack of a note has that note's scale degree, its figured
bass, its intervals to the other voices. In particular a later position asking
for a *different* note than the run started on. Another `kern` pitch, another
`deg` simply doesn't match, however neatly the durations would have divided the
onset up.

A run consumes as many positions as it needs from a single onset, and the
pattern carries on from the next onset. A match's `endLine`/`endPosition`
therefore report the last onset it actually consumed, which may be fewer onsets
than the pattern has positions.


### Combining it with `durationAllowSplitNotes`

The two options describe opposite mismatches between a pattern and the score
(one note written as several, several notes written as one) and never the same
one, so switching both on simply allows both. At each position a split run is
tried first; only if none is found does the position start a merged run instead.



## `metweightSkipUnclassified`

**Type:** `boolean` · **Default:** `false`

The two `duration` options above are about a pattern and a score spelling the
*same* notes out differently. This one is about the score having notes the
pattern doesn't mention at all: the **diminution** a chorale decorates a line
with. A neighbour note, an anticipation, a leap away and back: each of them puts
an extra onset between the notes that carry the phrase, and a pattern written
for the plain version then walks straight past it.

Setting `metweightSkipUnclassified: true` treats an onset the walked voice
attacks on a metrically **unclassified** position (its
[`metweight`](/docs/features/metweight) is `"u"`, i.e. between the beats the
meter classifies) as an **ornament** rather than as a note of its own. The
search then sees the voice with its ornaments folded away:

- the pattern never gets offered that onset at all;
- its **duration** falls to the note it decorates, the one in front of it;
- the **`mint`** into the *next* note is measured across it, from the note
  before it, rather than out of the ornament as the `**mint` spine writes it.

```json
{
    "feature": "kern",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "pattern": [
        { "mint": "*", "duration": "2" },
        { "mint": "-M2", "duration": "*", "fermata": true }
    ],
    "metweightSkipUnclassified": true
}
```

A half note stepping down into the fermata. In chor008 the soprano writes bar 4
as `4.b- 8a- 2a-;`: the eighth `a-` is an **anticipation**, already the fermata
note, taking its time from the `b-` in front of it. Without the option nothing
matches there, since there is no half note and no `-M2` out of one. With it, the
`b-` gets its eighth back and is the half note the phrase is made of, and the
step into the fermata is the `-M2` the pattern asked for.

A **neighbour note** folds away the same way. chor005's soprano reaches its
bar-4 fermata as `8a 8g 4a 4g;`, where the offbeat `g` is a lower neighbour:
with the ornament gone the two `a`s are adjacent, so the plain skeleton reaches
it, either as a quarter and its repetition, or, adding
[`durationAllowSplitNotes`](#durationallowsplitnotes), as the one half note the
query above asks for.

### It is the pattern that gets simpler, not looser

The option doesn't widen what a position matches; it changes which onsets there
are to match against. Everything a position asks for is still asked of the notes
that remain, and a plain, undiminished passage is unaffected, having no
unclassified onsets to fold away. So one pattern finds the plain reading and the
diminished one at once:

```json
{
    "feature": "kern",
    "voices": "bass",
    "pattern": [
        { "mint": "*", "duration": "4" },
        { "mint": "+2", "duration": "4" },
        { "mint": "-P5", "duration": "*", "fermata": true }
    ],
    "metweightSkipUnclassified": true,
    "mintAllowIntervalComplementation": "5"
}
```

A scale-degree 4–5–1 bass cadence. chor006 has it twice: bar 8 plainly, and bar
6 diminished into 4–2–5–1, where an offbeat eighth drops a third away from the
`4` and leaps a fourth back up to the `5`. Only the plain one is found without
the option, both with it. Note that what the ornament is shaped like never
enters into it, a step, a leap or a return alike. All that counts is where it
falls.

### What is never an ornament

- A **rest**. `**metweight` calls every rest unclassified wherever it falls, so
  requiring a sounding note is what keeps rests as the boundaries they are.
- A voice's **first onset**. There is no note in front of it whose duration it
  could belong to.
- An onset in **another voice**. Which onsets fold away is decided per voice,
  for the voice being walked; a [`simultaneousWith`](/docs/simultaneous) group
  does its own folding for its own voice, and can switch the option on or off
  independently.

### The other side of the trade

Since an ornament is not an onset any more, a pattern position can no longer
*land* on one while the option is on. A pattern that asks for `{ "metweight":
"u" }`, or that spells the ornament out itself (`8a`, `8g`, `4a`, …), will find
nothing it used to find. That is the same pattern's job with the option off.
Switch it on to search the skeleton, off to search the surface.

Everything else about an onset is read where it always was, from its own line:
its `deg`, its `fb`, its `hint` intervals, its `fermata`, its `kern` pitch. Only
its `duration` grows, and only the `mint` of a note that directly follows an
ornament is recomputed. A note with no ornament in front of it keeps the
`**mint` spine's own token.

### Combining it with the `duration` options

This option and the two `duration` ones can all be on at once, and a fuller
diminution needs more than one of them. Skipping an ornament is what makes the
notes on either side of it neighbours, and that is the precondition for either
`duration` option to reach across it:

- [`durationAllowSplitNotes`](#durationallowsplitnotes) can then sum a note the
  score both split into repetitions *and* decorated in between, into the one
  longer note it stands for. chor005's bar 4, above, is the case: the two `a`s
  only become a run of repetitions once the neighbour note between them is gone.
- [`durationAllowMergedNotes`](#durationallowmergednotes) can then spread a note
  an ornament just handed its time to across several pattern positions.
  chor008's bar 4 is the case: the `b-` has to grow to a half note before it can
  answer two quarter-note positions.



## `simultaneousAlignment`

**Type:** `string` · **Default:** `"start"` · **One of:** `"start"`, `"end"`, `"start-end"`

Only relevant with [`simultaneousWith`](/docs/simultaneous). Picks which of a
group's own match positions must line up with the primary match's:

- `"start"` — the group's match must **start** where the primary match starts.
- `"end"` — the group's match must **end** where the primary match ends.
- `"start-end"` — both at once, i.e. the two matches run for the exact same duration.

```json
{
    "feature": "deg",
    "voices": "soprano",
    "pattern": [
```
